World Cup Brings UMKC Students from Algeria a Sense of Home

Ph.D. students Lyza Iamrache and Said Tabharit are excited to welcome visitors from Algeria to Kansas City as the World Cup arrives.

Two UMKC international students will get a rare and powerful connection to home this summer, as Kansas City hosts the FIFA World Cup and Team Algeria takes the field just miles away from campus.

For Lyza Iamrache and Said Tabharit, both Ph.D. students in statistics, the 2026 World Cup is more than a global sporting event. It is a once-in-a-lifetime moment where their academic journeys and national pride intersect.

A meaningful connection to home

Iamrache and Tabharit came to Kansas City to continue their studies, joining a campus community whose international students represent more than 85 countries. They expected demanding coursework and a new city. They did not expect a global event to bring Algeria so close to them.

Algeria's appearance on an international stage offers more than a match to watch. It creates a reason to call family, share familiar food and explain traditions to classmates who may be encountering Algerian culture for the first time.

That exchange is part of what attracted both students to UMKC. A public research university in the center of the city gives them opportunities to build academic relationships while taking part in the life of a growing international destination.

Sharing a piece of Algeria

For Iamrache, welcoming visitors begins with conversation. She hopes people who come for a match will leave curious about Algeria's history, languages and regional traditions. Even a simple question about the team can become an opening for a deeper connection.

“The match gives us an opportunity to share our culture and help our friends understand why this moment matters.”

— Lyza Iamrache, UMKC News interview

Student organizations are considering informal watch parties and ways to help classmates understand the tournament. The goal is not simply to support one team, but to create an invitation that feels welcoming to people who know little about the sport or the country.

Tabharit sees the event as a bridge between communities. Kansas City is preparing to welcome visitors from around the world, while students who already call the region home can help those visitors understand the city beyond the stadium.

Kansas City becomes the meeting point

The tournament will bring new attention to neighborhoods, restaurants and cultural institutions throughout the metropolitan area. For UMKC students, that attention creates opportunities to volunteer, practice languages and introduce visitors to the place where they study.

“For a few weeks, two places that are important to us will feel much closer together.”

— Said Tabharit, UMKC News interview

The excitement also comes with practical questions. Students and visitors will need current transportation information, clear campus directions and accessible ways to find public events. University teams are working with regional partners to connect audiences with reliable information as plans develop.

Those details matter because hospitality is more than a welcome sign. It is the experience visitors have when they ask for help, navigate an unfamiliar neighborhood or join a celebration for the first time.

A moment that lasts beyond the match

No matter what happens on the field, Iamrache and Tabharit hope the connections formed during the tournament endure. A classmate who joins a watch party may become a friend. A visitor who explores the city may return. A young fan may begin imagining an international education.

“The strongest part of a global event is discovering how much people can share, even when they arrive with different stories.”

— Lyza Iamrache, UMKC News interview

For two students far from where they grew up, the World Cup offers an unusual overlap of home, study and city. For the UMKC community, it is a chance to listen, learn and participate in a global story unfolding close to campus.
